# Choose the presentation, then engineer it

Make a recommendation, not a menu of fashionable effects. Identify the visitor's question, the information's shape (one object, alternatives, sequence, relationship, human evidence), available truthful assets and the reader's likely attention. Choose the least elaborate format that fully communicates the claim; simplicity is not a license for weak composition or placeholder media.

## Selection table

| Information / visitor question | Preferred starting format | Art direction and engineering | Reject when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One result or object: “What does this give me?” | Carefully composed still, native UI crop or single product scene | Set focal subject, crop, readable scale, negative space for copy, background temperature and one dominant light/material. Render meaningful data, not tiny whole dashboards. | A still conceals the causal change being claimed, or the hero image teaches the wrong product category. |
| Several genuinely different outcomes: “What can the suite do?” | Curated gallery or asymmetric product spread | Give each frame a distinct job and media treatment; rank importance through area. Use a coherent gutter/caption system, not identical icon cards. On phone use legible stacked highlights or an accessible, labelled snap gallery with visible continuation and a non-hidden suite summary. | Frames are interchangeable or unrelated; required information exists only in an auto-advancing slide. |
| One action causes one visible outcome: “How does it work?” | Short bounded loop or replayable DOM scene | Write start → action → response → readable hold → reset. Explain the reset. Poster already communicates the outcome. Pause/offscreen suspension/reduced-motion state are part of the design. A proposed 6–10s loop is a testable starting budget, not a rule. | No meaningful state changes, fake execution, illegible timing, or competing simultaneous motion. |
| Ordered dependent steps: “How do these products connect?” | Continuous artifact with explicit step controls; optionally scroll-led enhancement | Preserve identity, amount, user selections and chronology. Specify entry/reverse/interruption/resize. Use a stable media stage so change is legible. Phone often becomes stacked key states; short viewports must not trap the story. | Artificial pinning, forced scroll duration, lost state, hidden final state or ordinary cards fading in being passed off as storytelling. |
| Parallel choices requiring comparison | Tabs with a stable evidence stage, comparison stills or an explorable gallery | Let the user control pace; name states semantically and preserve focus. Keep enough common geometry to perceive differences. | Tabs hide the very breadth the section needs to establish, or the content is sequential rather than alternative. |
| Human trust, setting or customer voice: “Is this for a business like mine?” | Placed documentary photograph or user-initiated customer film | Specify actual subject/action, authentic setting, composition, rights/release, matching grade and readable poster/caption. A film needs a credible speaker/story, not just motion. | Stock-like mood imagery substitutes for product proof; video is the only source of a key claim; real customer evidence is unavailable. |
| Architecture, ownership or fan-out: “What connects to what?” | Authored SVG/DOM diagram, possibly one guided highlight sequence | Use labels, edges and grouping to explain actual relationships. Keep necessary labels visible in fallback. Motion follows causality; it doesn't decorate every connector. | A globe, node cloud or orbit supplies atmosphere without explaining the architecture. |

## Stagger is choreography, not a format

Choose stagger only when the order helps the eye follow a real relationship or establishes a controlled reveal. Define item order, shared origin, overlap and readable end state. Do not impose a generic fade-up on every section. Long repeated staggers slow scanning; positional motion on body text usually contributes less than well-composed static type. Keep the page useful before animation and during fast scroll.

## Compose a media rhythm

Plan adjacent sections together. Change at least one meaningful dimension when the argument changes: scale, density, framing, subject or still/motion. Keep type, alignment and grading as anchors. A page full of equal video boxes is as monotonous as equal icon cards. Allow quiet passages after a demanding scene. Prefer one attention-leading motion in a viewport; justify and test exceptions.

Business and developer modes may show the same underlying operation with different evidence. A business view should expose outcome, money state and operational continuity. A developer view may expose request, object, event and operational tool. Do not invent APIs or show pseudo-success as actual execution. Validate vocabulary against approved capability evidence.

## Mandatory decision card

For every major scene provide: claim; selected format and why; rejected alternative and why; exact content/authority; dominant composition and surrounding material; desktop size/crop; phone recomposition; state/timing if moving; user control; poster/fallback; loading/resource strategy; acceptance capture/test. Label unverified capabilities and unavailable assets as blockers for that scene, not an invitation to fabricate them.

When media is not ready, specify its production brief or use an honest reduced-scope proof. Do not “finish” by filling the slot with generic illustration. For costly film or 3D, recommend the direction and identify the commissioning boundary without assuming permission to purchase or contact anyone.

## Calibration examples

Figma's square output gallery is appropriate to expressive breadth; adding a border/radius shell to every artwork would change the material argument. Mollie's warm live-business imagery places payments in context, while a white product dashboard supplies a different kind of evidence. Stripe's differently sized product scenes explain breadth through concrete objects, not identical product-name tiles. Shopify can pair a dominant product demonstration with a narrower customer film because demonstration and testimony answer different questions. Linear's layered native-looking artifacts suit technical workflow explanation; its fade masks are not a license to hide required amount or status text.

These are dated reference interpretations (11 October 2026), not conversion evidence or instructions to reuse assets.
