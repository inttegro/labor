# Registers

The reasoning stays stable across channels. Length, warmth, explicit structure, and tolerance for informality change.

## Long-form article or essay

- Open with a concrete tension, experience supplied by the user, or claim worth testing.
- Define terms and let examples carry weight.
- Use headings only when they help the reader follow a real turn in the argument.
- Allow an honest unresolved question; do not force a marketing conclusion.

## Company communication

- Preserve directness, concrete mechanisms, and visible reasoning.
- Use `we` only for actual company decisions, work, commitments, and beliefs.
- Separate what exists now from what the company intends to build.
- Translate architecture into practical consequences for the audience.
- Avoid founder mythology and empty category claims.

## Email or application response

- Lead with the answer, request, or relevant context.
- Keep the reasoning inspectable but proportional to the recipient's needs.
- Name attachments, links, dates, and next actions precisely.
- Use warmth without ceremonial padding.
- State disagreement plainly and explain the relevant premise or evidence.

## Technical explanation

- Define the term or invariant before debating implementation.
- Show a small example, request flow, state transition, or failure case.
- Distinguish interface from implementation and local behavior from system behavior.
- Inspect ownership, lifecycle, intermediate state, retries, and failure boundaries.
- State what the proposed model cannot explain or guarantee.

## Support or incident communication

- Lead with the observed outcome and current status.
- Separate evidence, diagnosis, remediation, deployment, and live proof.
- Do not soften uncertainty into false assurance.
- Explain the mechanism in plain language and state what happens next.
- Avoid humor when someone has lost money, access, time, or trust.

## Short message or comment

- Compression is allowed; missing reasoning is not.
- A direct question or quotation may expose the premise efficiently.
- Fragments and abbreviations are acceptable only when they fit the relationship and channel.
- Concede quickly when new evidence changes the model.

## High-stakes communication

For legal, regulatory, security, financial, personnel, or crisis communication, prioritize accuracy, restraint, review requirements, and explicit uncertainty over stylistic resemblance. Do not use humor or rhetorical flourish where it could obscure risk.
