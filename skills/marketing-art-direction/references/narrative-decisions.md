# Narrative and decisions

A suite homepage is not an org chart. Start with a recognized problem, explain the connected mechanism, show consequences, then provide relevant entry points. Product count is not a benefit.

For each chapter: “The visitor now believes ___ because they saw ___; the next question is ___.” Merge redundant chapters. Remove blocks adding no evidence, decision or emotional meaning.

Map audience needs to verified capabilities, not internal service names. A transaction can introduce a suite without defining it as retail-only. Bridge to money, operations, relationships and extensibility where verified. Distinguish available, constrained, invitation-only, future and illustrative.

## Conversion

Choose an action the business can fulfil. Clarify next step, audience and material eligibility. Request access without an owner or response process is unfinished. Keep useful learning routes for visitors not ready to act.

Match proof to objections: real UI for usability, verified workflow for integration, authorized case evidence for outcomes, applicable documentation for security/coverage. Never invent logos, metrics, endorsements or availability.

Visual clarity may plausibly improve comprehension, but does not prove conversion uplift. Later test category comprehension, suite recognition, finding the right route and completing the real funnel.

## Transfer mechanisms

Linear: shared product objects and foreground/background hierarchy.
Figma: varied outputs unified by an editorial frame.
Mollie: recognizable business action and specific financial outcomes.
Retool: continuity between explanation and visual, plus institutional-risk proof.

Do not assemble their brand costumes. Choose one grammar and adapt mechanisms.

Critique selected work using Before / Proposed refinement / Why, with evidence. Separate strategic gaps, composition gaps and execution gaps. Don't decorate an unresolved category problem. Don't reopen the approved direction because an outside skill dislikes its palette.
