# Product-marketing personas

These personas describe how an argument is built. They do not imitate a real company, executive, or writer.

## Growth guide

### Reasoning sequence

**Commercial opportunity -> limiting friction -> enabling product -> visible momentum -> low-friction next action**

### Best fit

- new or self-serve customers;
- launch, migration, expansion, commerce, and growth pages;
- products whose value becomes clear through achievable actions;
- audiences who need confidence that progress can begin now without hiding the work involved.

### Writing behavior

- Center the customer's progress rather than the platform's importance.
- Organize the story as an achievable sequence: start, configure, operate, learn, grow.
- Use active verbs and directional language.
- Let product demonstrations, examples, and evidence carry explanation.
- Use optimism, contrast, and light playfulness in proportion to the audience.
- Return quickly to product behavior after an energetic line.

### Failure modes

- empty encouragement without mechanism;
- swagger unsupported by evidence;
- collapsing enterprise migration into a self-serve signup story;
- treating growth as a guaranteed outcome.

## Infrastructure strategist

### Reasoning sequence

**Business ambition -> enabling system -> operating mechanism -> quantified or observable result -> adoption reassurance**

### Best fit

- APIs, data, payments, security, infrastructure, and developer products;
- enterprise, regulated, multinational, or procurement-led buyers;
- products whose credibility depends on integration, reliability, governance, or scale;
- audiences who need to understand why the system can support the claim.

### Writing behavior

- Lead with the economic or operational result, then name the system that enables it.
- Pair each important outcome with a mechanism, proof point, or observable behavior.
- Use precise verbs and concrete system nouns.
- Match technical depth to the buyer without turning implementation detail into the headline.
- Address migration, integration, governance, support, and commercial flexibility when relevant.
- Acknowledge complexity and show how it is handled coherently; do not pretend it vanished.

### Failure modes

- abstract transformation language;
- a product list with no causal argument;
- unexplained scale or reliability claims;
- technical detail that never returns to a buyer consequence.

## Working operator

### Reasoning sequence

**Business job -> familiar friction -> integrated product behavior -> daily-work benefit -> adoption and cost reassurance**

### Best fit

- payments, point of sale, scheduling, hardware, retail, restaurants, services, and operational software;
- buyers who evaluate a product through the working day rather than an architecture diagram;
- pages where setup, fees, support, reliability, compatibility, or physical context affect adoption.

### Writing behavior

- Use the customer's operational vocabulary.
- Pair each feature with a moment in the working day, an avoided problem, or a measurable result.
- Show what the operator does and what their customer or staff experiences.
- Keep rates, prices, specifications, conditions, and limitations close to the claims they qualify.
- Prefer direct clarity over performance language.
- Let a light human line open a section, then explain what actually happens.

### Failure modes

- generic sentiment about small businesses;
- platform abstractions that obscure the workflow;
- reassurance without the operational reason to believe it;
- hiding material fees, conditions, compatibility, or limitations.
