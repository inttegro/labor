# Page modes

Select only the mode that fits the requested surface. The chosen persona still governs the argument.

## Launch or start

- Reduce the distance between intent and a functioning outcome.
- Organize the story as a sequence of achievable actions.
- Prefer tangible customer actions to broad platform descriptions.
- Include future readiness only when the present path genuinely supports it.

## Migration or switch

- Name the limitation the buyer already understands.
- Address continuity, risk, timing, data, support, and reversibility.
- Support comparative claims with defined evidence.
- Make the migration mechanism part of the product proposition.

## Product

- Open with the product's most important outcome.
- Explain the mechanism or behavior that produces it.
- Demonstrate performance, control, extensibility, or operational breadth instead of listing features.
- Move from the core job to adjacent capabilities only when they strengthen the same argument.

## Enterprise

- Lead with an executive-level proposition, not a feature inventory.
- Attach outcomes to mechanisms and defensible evidence.
- Address integration, migration, governance, procurement, support, and commercial flexibility.
- Use customer or analyst proof only when supplied and authorized.

## Startup or early-stage team

- Follow the operating lifecycle: build, launch, learn, monetize, scale.
- Emphasize speed and optionality without implying that scale is automatic.
- Recognize the actual business model rather than speaking to a generic startup.
- Include credits, community, or advisory help only when real.

## Industry or use case

- Begin with the category's economic or operating reality.
- Use the industry's vocabulary and characteristic workflow, money flow, risk, or constraint.
- Select only relevant capabilities and explain how they work together.
- Avoid swapping industry nouns into a generic product page.

## Hardware or physical product

- Put the product, primary use, price, and purchase action early.
- Explain performance, durability, connectivity, compatibility, and software through real scenarios.
- State included components, accessories, taxes, and offline limitations accurately.
- Let imagery show appearance; use copy for decisions and consequences.

## Pricing

- Reduce metaphor and narrative.
- State the commercial model and expose material fees, thresholds, conditions, and regional differences.
- Help the buyer identify the correct path.
- Separate standard self-serve adoption from negotiated or complex arrangements.

## Customer story

- Make the customer the protagonist.
- Establish their operating model and constraint before introducing the product.
- Explain the decision, implementation, changed workflow, and measured result.
- Separate verified results from attributed testimony.
